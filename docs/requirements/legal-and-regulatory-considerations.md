# Legal and Regulatory Considerations

## 1. Important limitation

DEPOLEDGER is a technical architecture proposal, not legal advice, a regulatory classification, or evidence of supervisory approval. The bank's qualified legal, compliance, risk, product, and regulatory teams must assess each product, jurisdiction, participant, and deployment model before customer use.

## 2. Questions requiring institution-specific review

- What legal claim and contractual rights does the token represent, and who owes the obligation?
- How do transfer, redemption, withdrawal, maturity, holds, disputes, and insolvency operate under applicable law and executed terms?
- Are deposit-protection arrangements applicable, and what disclosures are required?
- What are the requirements for customer identification, beneficial ownership, AML, sanctions, fraud monitoring, record keeping, and suspicious-activity handling?
- What privacy, data localization, retention, deletion, legal hold, cross-border transfer, and regulator access obligations apply?
- What accounting, prudential, liquidity, capital, tax, and regulatory reporting treatment applies?
- What payment-system, settlement, network participation, outsourcing, operational resilience, and cyber-security approvals apply?
- What legal and governance requirements apply to brokered, structured, interbank, or Islamic deposit structures?
- How are external settlement status and technical ledger finality distinguished from contractual and legal finality?

## 3. India-specific review

For an Indian bank, qualified institutional reviewers should assess applicable Reserve Bank of India requirements, payment-system rules, data-protection and cybersecurity obligations, deposit-taking and customer-protection requirements, record retention, and supervisory obligations. Applicability depends on the actual product, parties, architecture, and operating model; this document does not determine it.

## 4. Evidence and governance

Maintain a decision register linking each legal/regulatory conclusion to the product version, jurisdiction, accountable reviewer, approval date, source materials, conditions, and re-review triggers. Reassess when product terms, participant topology, data flows, settlement model, or applicable rules change. Do not represent a requirement as approved or implemented without verifiable evidence.
